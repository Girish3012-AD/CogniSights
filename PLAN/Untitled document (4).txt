⚠️ One VERY important distinction
Don't conclude:
Earth-OneVision = best model, therefore use Earth-OneVision.
That's not the correct engineering conclusion.
For example, current benchmark evidence shows that general RS-MLLMs can be beaten by specialists on individual capabilities. On the RSVG benchmark, a specialist reaches 84.48 P@0.5 while Earth-OneVision is 65.06; meanwhile on VRSBench, older models such as GeoChat remain competitive on some individual tasks.

🚨 And I would add 5 capabilities to our research checklist
These weren't emphasized enough in your original list, but they could become major differentiators:
1. 🔎 Active perception
"I will zoom/crop and inspect before answering."
2. 🕒 Temporal intelligence
"I understand what changed, not just what exists."
3. 🧮 Geospatial computation
"I can calculate area, distance, proximity, intersection, density, etc."
4. 🧾 Evidence + uncertainty
"Here is what I observed, where I observed it, how confident I am, and what I cannot establish."
5. 🤖 Autonomous tool selection
"I decide whether this question needs VLM, grounding, segmentation, temporal analysis, GIS, retrieval, or another specialist."
That fifth one is especially important because it turns SatQuery from a model into a system.

🎯 The next thing we should do
Don't jump to architecture yet.
The correct next deliverable is a massive Model-by-Model Autopsy Matrix containing roughly 50 serious models/systems with columns for:
Parameters → architecture → backbone → modalities → image resolution → temporal support → VQA → captioning → counting → grounding → HBB → OBB → polygon → segmentation → change detection → SAR → multispectral → IR → video → multi-image → GIS → geographic context → reasoning → agentic behavior → hallucination handling → active perception → datasets → training size → benchmark scores → GPU/compute → inference requirements → open-source status → license → checkpoint availability → strengths → weaknesses → unique innovation → SatQuery usefulness.
That will give us the actual evidence base from which we can derive SatQuery's capabilities, instead of guessing features.
And importantly, we should include both generalists and specialists—because the current evidence clearly shows that a general RS-VLM can be excellent overall while a specialist can still dominate a particular capability.
