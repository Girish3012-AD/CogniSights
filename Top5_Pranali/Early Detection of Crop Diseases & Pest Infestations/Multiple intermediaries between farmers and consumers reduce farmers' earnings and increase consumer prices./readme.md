26033 — Agricultural Supply Chain
1. Problem Statement Details
   
Problem Statement ID: 26033

Problem Statement Title: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers' earnings and increase consumer prices.

Organization: Government of Maharashtra

Department: Department of Agriculture, Government of Maharashtra

Category: Software

Theme: Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

1. Problem Statement

The agricultural supply chain often involves multiple intermediaries between farmers and final consumers. A farmer may sell produce to a local trader, who may sell it to another wholesaler, distributor or retailer before it finally reaches the consumer.

Because of these multiple intermediaries, the farmer may receive a relatively low price for the produce, while the final consumer may have to pay a much higher price.

The problem is further increased by:

Lack of direct connection between farmers and buyers
Inefficient transportation and logistics
Poor demand forecasting
Supply-demand mismatch
Post-harvest losses
Lack of price transparency
High transportation and handling costs

The result is a situation where farmers earn less while consumers pay more.

2. Main Problem

The core problem can be represented as:
Farmer → Trader → Wholesaler → Distributor → Retailer → Consumer
At every stage, additional costs and margins can be added.

This can create:
Low farmer income + High consumer price + Supply-chain inefficiency
The challenge is to reduce unnecessary intermediaries and make the agricultural supply chain more direct and efficient.

3. Proposed Solution
AI-Powered Digital Agricultural Marketplace
We propose a digital marketplace that directly connects:
Farmers/FPOs ↔ Consumers/Bulk Buyers
The platform will allow farmers and FPOs to list their agricultural products directly, while consumers, retailers and bulk buyers can discover and purchase those products.

AI can then be used to improve the supply chain by:
Forecasting demand
Matching supply with demand
Optimizing transportation routes
Supporting logistics planning
Identifying high-demand locations
Reducing unnecessary transportation
Improving price transparency
4. How the Solution Works

Step 1 — Farmer/FPO Registration

Farmers or FPOs register on the platform and provide information such as:
Crop/product
Quantity available
Expected price
Harvest date
Location
Quality/grade
Step 2 — Product Listing

The farmer lists available produce on the marketplace.
Example:
Product: Tomato
Quantity: 2,000 kg
Location: Nashik
Harvest Date: 25 August
Expected Price: ₹X/kg

Step 3 — Buyer Searches
Consumers, retailers and bulk buyers can search for required products.
They can compare:
Price
Quantity
Location
Quality
Availability

Step 4 — AI Demand Forecasting

The AI model analyzes historical and current data to predict future demand.
It can consider:
Historical sales
Seasonal demand
Crop availability
Market trends
Location
Previous orders

Example:
AI predicts that tomato demand in a particular city will increase next week.
This information can help farmers and buyers plan supply.

Step 5 — Supply-Demand Matching
The system matches available agricultural products with suitable buyers.

Farmer Supply
      ↓
AI Matching Engine
      ↓
Buyer Demand
      ↓
Best Possible Match
Step 6 — Logistics Optimization

After an order is placed, the system can optimize transportation.

It can consider:

Distance
Delivery locations
Vehicle capacity
Number of orders
Delivery time
Transportation cost

The goal is to find an efficient route and reduce unnecessary transportation costs.

Step 7 — Delivery

The produce is transported from the farmer/FPO to the buyer or consumer.

5. AI Components
