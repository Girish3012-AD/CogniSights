# PASS 3: Factor Validation

This document validates the candidate factors used to evaluate the 172 SIH 2026 problem statements. Every factor is treated as a hypothesis to ensure we are tracking meaningful, independent dimensions without double-counting.

## A. SCALE
**Affected Population**
- **Relevance**: High. Software solving a problem for millions has higher impact.
- **Evidence strength**: Often relies on estimates or census proxies.
- **Measurability**: Medium. Difficult to get precise numbers for niche issues.
- **Independence**: High.
- **Predictive usefulness**: High.
- **Reliability**: Medium (high risk of inflation by problem authors).
- **Bias risk**: Bias towards urban or general problems over critical niche industrial problems.
- **Double-counting risk**: Low.
- **Factor Confidence**: [MEDIUM]

**Geographic Reach**
- **Relevance**: Medium. Global vs National vs Regional.
- **Evidence strength**: Easy to determine from the problem scope.
- **Measurability**: High.
- **Independence**: Low (often overlaps with Affected Population).
- **Predictive usefulness**: Medium.
- **Reliability**: High.
- **Bias risk**: Low.
- **Double-counting risk**: High (overlaps with Affected Population).
- **Factor Confidence**: [MEDIUM] - Use as secondary modifier to Population.

## B. INTENSITY
**Severity**
- **Relevance**: High. Life-threatening vs inconvenience.
- **Evidence strength**: Qualitative, based on case studies or incident reports.
- **Measurability**: Low.
- **Independence**: High.
- **Predictive usefulness**: High.
- **Reliability**: Medium.
- **Bias risk**: Author sensationalism.
- **Double-counting risk**: Low.
- **Factor Confidence**: [HIGH]

**Frequency**
- **Relevance**: High. Daily occurrence vs once a decade.
- **Evidence strength**: Statistical logs (e.g., crime rates, landslide frequencies).
- **Measurability**: High.
- **Independence**: High.
- **Predictive usefulness**: High.
- **Reliability**: High.
- **Bias risk**: Low.
- **Double-counting risk**: Low.
- **Factor Confidence**: [HIGH]

## C. BURDEN

- **Relevance**: High. Drives willingness to adopt.

- **Independence**: High.

**Time Burden**

- **Evidence strength**: High (can be timed via workflow analysis).
- **Measurability**: High.
- **Independence**: High.
- **Predictive usefulness**: High.
- **Reliability**: High.
- **Bias risk**: Low.
- **Double-counting risk**: Low.
- **Factor Confidence**: [HIGH]

## D. GAP

- **Relevance**: Critical. Determines the "unmet need".
- **Evidence strength**: Market analysis and product reviews.
- **Measurability**: Medium (qualitative).
- **Independence**: High.
- **Predictive usefulness**: Very High.
- **Reliability**: Medium.
- **Bias risk**: Medium (ignoring competitors is common).
- **Double-counting risk**: Low.
- **Factor Confidence**: [HIGH]

**Adoption Gap**
- **Relevance**: High. Why aren't existing solutions used?

- **Independence**: Medium (overlaps with affordability/accessibility).
- **Predictive usefulness**: High.
- **Reliability**: Low.
- **Bias risk**: High.
- **Double-counting risk**: Medium.
- **Factor Confidence**: [LOW]

### Summary of Validated Factors for Multi-Dimensional Scoring
1. **Real-world impact**: Severity + Frequency + Population [EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED]
2. **Opportunity**: Existing Solution Weakness + Economic/Time Burden [FACT]
3. **Systemic leverage**: Bottleneck Potential + Downstream Effects [INFERENCE]
4. **Future potential**: Growth Trajectory [ESTIMATE]
5. **Feasibility**: Data Availability + Tech Complexity [FACT]
6. **Scalability**: Marginal Cost + Deployment Constraints [FACT]
7. **SIH competitiveness**: Demonstrability + Innovation [HYPOTHESIS]
